# Third-Party Vendor Continuous Monitoring Plan

## 1. Purpose

This plan establishes ongoing oversight requirements for MedCloud Solutions, the proposed provider of cloud-based patient record management software.

Monitoring activities are intended to identify changes in vendor risk, track remediation commitments, verify continued compliance with security requirements, and support timely risk-based decisions throughout the vendor relationship.

## 2. Vendor Monitoring Profile

| Field | Details |
|---|---|
| Organization | Northstar Health Network |
| Vendor | MedCloud Solutions |
| Service | Cloud-based patient record management SaaS |
| Vendor Risk Tier | High |
| Data Sensitivity | High - Patient and sensitive information |
| Monitoring Frequency | Monthly remediation tracking; quarterly risk review; annual comprehensive reassessment |
| Monitoring Owner | Third-Party Risk Management / GRC |
| Approval Status | Production approval deferred pending risk treatment |

## 3. Monitoring Activities

| Activity | Frequency | Responsible Party | Evidence or Record |
|---|---|---|---|
| Review outstanding remediation actions | Monthly until closure | GRC Analyst | Updated remediation tracker and overdue action log |
| Review vendor security incidents and material disclosures | Ongoing; formal monthly review | Vendor Security Contact and GRC | Incident notifications and documented impact assessments |
| Review changes to the service, hosting arrangements, or data processing | Upon material change | Vendor Relationship Owner and GRC | Change notification and updated service assessment |
| Review subcontractor and subprocessor changes | Upon change; formal quarterly review | Third-Party Risk Management | Updated subprocessor inventory and due diligence records |
| Review vulnerability and patch management assurance | Quarterly | Security Team / GRC | Vulnerability summaries, patch compliance metrics, and remediation evidence |
| Review access security and MFA assurance | Quarterly | Security Team / GRC | Access review summaries and MFA coverage evidence |
| Review incident response and recovery readiness | Annually and after significant changes or incidents | Security and Business Continuity Teams | Exercise reports and recovery test results |
| Review independent security assurance | Annually or upon material change | GRC Analyst | Current assurance report, scope review, and identified exceptions |
| Conduct comprehensive vendor risk reassessment | Annually, or earlier if triggered | Third-Party Risk Management | Updated questionnaire, evidence review, and risk assessment |

## 4. Key Monitoring Indicators

The following indicators should be tracked where relevant data is available:

| Indicator | Monitoring Objective | Escalation Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Overdue Critical remediation actions | Ensure urgent security gaps receive timely attention | Any Critical action passes its approved due date |
| Overdue High remediation actions | Track significant unresolved security weaknesses | A High action passes its due date without an approved exception |
| Critical vulnerability remediation | Evaluate handling of severe vulnerabilities | An agreed remediation deadline is missed |
| MFA coverage | Confirm required accounts and access paths are protected | Required MFA coverage cannot be demonstrated or a material gap is identified |
| Security incident notification | Confirm timely communication of incidents affecting the service or data | A material incident is reported or contractual notification requirements may have been breached |
| Backup restoration and recovery testing | Evaluate service recoverability | A required test fails or evidence is not provided when due |
| Subprocessor inventory currency | Identify changes in downstream data handling | An unreviewed subprocessor change is identified |
| Independent assurance status | Maintain appropriate security assurance | Required assurance expires, contains material exceptions, or is unavailable |

Escalation triggers should be aligned with contractual commitments, the organization's policies, and approved risk tolerances.

## 5. Monitoring and Escalation Process

1. Request and review vendor updates and evidence according to the monitoring schedule.
2. Record findings, evidence references, control status, action owners, and target dates.
3. Compare new information with the initial risk assessment and previously recorded remediation commitments.
4. Escalate material incidents, overdue Critical actions, significant control failures, and material changes to the designated risk owner and relevant stakeholders.
5. Determine whether a targeted reassessment or comprehensive reassessment is required.
6. Document decisions, accepted exceptions, and follow-up actions.

## 6. Reassessment Triggers

A reassessment should be initiated when one or more of the following occurs:

- A security incident materially affects the vendor service or Northstar Health Network data.
- A material change occurs in hosting, architecture, data processing, or service functionality.
- A new subcontractor receives access to sensitive information or materially changes the service's risk profile.
- Significant vulnerabilities or repeated control failures are identified.
- A required assurance report reveals material exceptions.
- The vendor fails to meet important remediation commitments.
- Applicable legal, regulatory, contractual, or organizational requirements change.
- The scheduled annual reassessment becomes due.

## 7. Evidence and Recordkeeping

Monitoring records should include:

- Date of review and reviewer.
- Vendor contact or source of the information.
- Evidence reviewed and its relevant period or scope.
- Findings, limitations, and control status.
- Risk rating changes and supporting rationale.
- Remediation actions, owners, and deadlines.
- Escalations and approval decisions.

Evidence should be stored in an access-controlled repository according to Northstar Health Network's record-retention requirements. Sensitive vendor documents should be handled under appropriate confidentiality and data protection controls.

## 8. Vendor Status and Relationship Decisions

The vendor's monitoring status should reflect the current risk position and management decision:

- **Pending Approval:** Required due diligence or approval conditions remain outstanding.
- **Approved:** Required controls and approval criteria have been satisfied and authorization has been documented.
- **Conditionally Approved:** Explicit authorization permits a defined scope subject to documented safeguards, deadlines, and restrictions.
- **Suspended:** Use is temporarily restricted due to a material risk, incident, or breach of requirements.
- **Terminated:** The relationship has ended through an authorized decision.

A status change must be supported by a documented decision. Completion of remediation alone does not authorize production use.

## 9. Governance and Reporting

The GRC function should provide periodic summaries to the vendor relationship owner and designated risk owner. Reports should highlight risk-rating changes, overdue remediation, incidents, significant vendor changes, assurance gaps, and decisions requiring management attention.

Critical issues should be escalated promptly rather than waiting for the next scheduled report.

## 10. Review of This Plan

This plan should be reviewed at least annually and whenever material changes occur in the vendor relationship, service scope, risk profile, or applicable requirements.
